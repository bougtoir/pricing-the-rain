## Why should a JEBO reader care?

JEBO's remit is how "cognitive, computational and informational
characteristics" of agents shape economic outcomes. This paper formalizes a
channel squarely inside that remit: when the experience on which beliefs are
trained is generated by endogenous participation, beliefs are biased in a
signed, analyzable direction, and the bias transmits into prices and
reputations through specific, decomposable mechanisms. The contribution is
behavioral/information-economic in mechanism even though it contains no
human-subject data — the same structural position as theoretical papers JEBO
regularly publishes (e.g., Liu & Sanyal 2012; Jehiel-style misspecified
learning in this journal's adjacent outlets), and the paper says so
explicitly rather than pretending to behavioral evidence.

## Is the contribution behavioral enough for JEBO without pretending to human data?

Yes, with the calibration the manuscript already makes: the selection
identity and matched-support decomposition are information economics;
the subjective-transformation layer is presented as a parameterized
scenario compared against alternative learning rules; the quote rule is
reduced-form; and the real-weather benchmark is a controlled known-zero
demonstration of the selection mechanism, not behavioral evidence. The
paper's claim is about what a quote *can* encode, which is exactly the
kind of mechanism paper JEBO's "eclectic as to research method" scope
covers.
