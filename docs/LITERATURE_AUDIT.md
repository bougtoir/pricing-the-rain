# Literature and novelty audit

## Closest work

Espín-Sánchez, Gil-Guirado, and Ryan, “Praying for Rain” (*QJE*, 2026;
doi:10.1093/qje/qjag026), is the controlling comparator. It shows how ineffective
prayer can be persuasive when an increasing rainfall hazard interacts with drought
timing and a hidden counterfactual. It analyzes more than two centuries of Murcia
records and reports that, among 1,208 coded ethnic groups, groups facing an increasing
hazard are 47% more likely to practice rainmaking.

The QJE article also goes beyond timing alone. It models tangible rainfall benefits and
support costs, treats support as potentially including donations, discusses historical
offerings and payments, allows cultural selection of persuasive leaders and policies,
and explicitly considers strategic timing as an observationally equivalent alternative.
The previous audit understated this overlap. The corrected domain-by-domain comparison
is in `docs/QJE_DIFFERENTIATION_AUDIT.md`.

The defensible incremental question is whether a provider market and performance system
with separately modeled requests and acceptance can produce provider-specific prices
and persistent reputation under an exact rainfall-effect null—and whether those premia
remain informative about current apparent success once past attributed outcomes and
current ecological hazard are separated.

## Evidence domains

- **Rainfall hazard and prayer:** the QJE article and its NBER working paper are the
  primary comparator. Murcia context is also described in *Ecology and Society*.
- **Demand, benefit, cost, and support:** the QJE article already links rainfall demand
  to agriculture and formally includes tangible benefits and support costs. These
  generic elements are not novel here.
- **Historical rainmaking:** sources document diverse rituals, timing, sacrifices,
  offerings, and in at least one Pedi account a provider setting a price. Evidence is
  too heterogeneous to calibrate a universal market or drought-price escalation rule.
- **Attribution and regression to the mean:** classic judgment-under-uncertainty,
  reinforcement, and regression-to-the-mean literatures motivate observer models.
- **Reputation and social learning:** incomplete-information reputation and
  informational-cascade models motivate persistent provider differences.
- **Plural valuation:** ecological-economics work on cultural, shared, social, and
  relational values rejects equating monetary WTP with total value.
- **Drought valuation:** stated-preference studies establish that drought impacts and
  mitigation can carry economic value, but their estimates are not transferred into
  historical ritual prices here.
- **Weather data and drought:** ERA5/ERA5-Land and standardized drought-index work
  support the empirical hazard design.

## Historical-evidence conclusion

Historical evidence supports the existence of drought-associated ritual practice,
offerings, specialized practitioners, and context-specific compensation. It does **not**
support a general claim that practitioners systematically raised fees later in a dry
spell. The model therefore treats fee schedules and acceptance behavior as explicit
theory scenarios, not reconstructed history.

## QJE and supporting-material verification

The official article record, open-access article text, February 2026 NBER version,
Harvard Dataverse metadata, and the deposited bibliography files were checked. The
official article is CC BY 4.0; the Dataverse release is CC0 1.0. Direct automated
retrieval of the official OUP PDF returned HTTP 403, so the archived NBER PDF was used
for page-level comparison. The complete Dataverse metadata inventories the 1.07 GB
Atlas archive and seven smaller files. The large archive was not downloaded because
this audit does not reproduce the QJE estimates; that partial acquisition is stated in
the source ledger and is not described as a complete local replica.

## Other metadata verification

Crossref metadata was archived for 27 of 28 configured DOI records. The 1987 Akong'a
article is absent from Crossref; its institutional-repository PDF was archived and
checksummed instead.

The repository landing metadata describes the title as “A Comprehensive Study,” while
the archived article itself is headed “A Comparative Study.” The manuscript follows
the title printed in the primary institutional PDF and preserves this discrepancy in
the audit rather than silently reconciling it.

The audit detected three DOI–role mismatches before manuscript drafting:

- `10.1016/j.ecolecon.2013.07.004` is a capability-approach article, not the intended
  monetary-valuation critique.
- `10.1016/j.ecolecon.2017.11.006` concerns growth-versus-environment opinion, not
  integrated plural valuation.
- `10.1016/j.cosust.2017.12.005` concerns climate storytelling, not relational values.

They are marked excluded. Verified replacements are
`10.1016/j.ecolecon.2013.07.002`, `10.1016/j.ecoser.2016.11.007`, and
`10.1016/j.cosust.2018.09.005`. This correction is preserved in the acquisition
ledger and machine-readable metadata.

## Novelty verdict

**MODIFY AND GO, WITH A NARROWER CLAIM.** Endogenous timing, increasing hazards,
scarcity-dependent demand, generic benefits and costs, strategic timing, and persistence
of persuasive practices are not novel after the QJE article. The distinct contribution
is the request-to-acceptance selection problem, provider-specific price and reputation
formation, and the stock-flow result that reputation-mediated premia can coexist with
zero or negative contemporaneous price-success differences under a strict zero-effect
null. Pricing remains simulated, plural values are not collapsed into money, and the
eight locations remain non-representative mechanism stress tests.
