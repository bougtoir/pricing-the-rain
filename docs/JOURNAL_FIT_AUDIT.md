## Fit

The paper studies how a natural hazard process and scarcity interact with endogenous
demand, provider strategy, valuation, price, counterfactual attribution, and
reputation. Its ecological-economic contribution is not a test of religion. It is a
general account of how environmental variability can be converted into observed
performance and monetary valuation when interventions are selected in time and
counterfactuals are hidden.

## Desk-reject risks and design response

1. **Closest-prior-work risk:** the 2026 QJE article already links rain prayer to an
   increasing rainfall hazard. Response: treat that mechanism as the starting point,
   estimate rather than assume hazards, retain non-monotonic cases, and make pricing,
   plural valuation, strategic acceptance, and reputation the incremental contribution.
2. **Gimmick risk:** rainmaking could appear chosen for novelty. Response: state the
   general selection-and-valuation mechanism first and use rainmaking only as a
   transparent zero-effect application.
3. **Cultural-reductionism risk:** monetisation could flatten plural values. Response:
   separate instrumental rainfall value, belief-mediated WTP, social/ritual utility,
   and cultural meaning; do not estimate the last from weather.
4. **Pricing-identification risk:** no historical price series is available. Response:
   label every price result as a calibrated scenario and never claim historical
   identification.
5. **Obvious-regression-to-mean risk:** dry spells ending is insufficient novelty.
   Response: compare hazards, matched timings, strategic acceptance, observer models,
   and hazard-adjusted price associations across climates.
6. **Causal-language risk:** observational pseudo-events have no intervention effect by
   construction. Response: reserve “success” for apparent attribution and never call
   regression adjustment causal identification.

## Post-results recheck

The realized findings strengthen the journal fit through the explicit separation of
environmental scarcity, demand, strategic selection, transfers, instrumental value,
and social utility. They weaken any simple claim that higher prices reliably predict
better apparent outcomes. Final robustness checks do not support the declared positive
association: the clustered logit interval includes zero, and the within-stratum
permutation and M4–M5 associations are negative. The contribution therefore focuses
on how ecological variability enters economic valuation and reputational institutions
under hidden counterfactuals, not on a universal positive price slope.
